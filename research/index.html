Accelerator programming

SALTyRN

CGO 2027

Keagan Chern, Charles Hong, Sophia Shao, Alvin Cheung

Symbolically models RVV intrinsics for formal verification of Arm Neon ports. Then tunes translated kernels with Autocomp, yielding a 1.4x speedup over handwritten RVV across 35 kernels, with several upstreamed to XNNPACK.

JAXBench

EACL 2027

Arya Tschand*, Charles Hong*, Julian Walker, Nina Cai, Shangkun Wang, Suvinay Subramanian, Sundar Dev, Vijay Janapa Reddi, Amir Yazdanbakhsh, Sethu Sankaran
* Equal contribution

A comprehensive benchmark for agentic TPU kernel optimization. As an initial contribution, we demonstrate that Autocomp achieves a 1.36x geometric-mean speedup over XLA across JAXBench's 50 workloads.

Autocomp

MLArchSys 2025 Best Paper, ASPLOS 2026 Tutorial

Charles Hong, Sahil Bhatia, Alvin Cheung, Yakun Sophia Shao

The first agentic system to outperform humans in optimizing low-resource accelerator code. Delivers speedups of 5.6x over Gemmini's GEMM library, 1.9x over hand-tuned AWS Trainium kernels, and 3.8x over TVM on NVIDIA L40S.

LLM-Aided Compilation for Tensor Accelerators

ISLAD 2024

Charles Hong, Sahil Bhatia, Altan Haan, Shengjun Kris Dong, Dima Nikiforov, Alvin Cheung, Yakun Sophia Shao

The first paper to demonstrate successful use of LLMs to write low-resource accelerator code. Uses GPT-4 to "compile" robotics kernels to Gemmini, Berkeley's tensor accelerator.

Hardware design

hdl2v

MLCAD 2025

Charles Hong, Brendan Roberts, Huijae An, Alex Um, Advay Ratan, Yakun Sophia Shao

A Verilog dataset constructed by translating designs from VHDL, Chisel, and PyMTL3. Fine-tuning a 32B-parameter code model with hdl2v improves VerilogEvalV2 pass@10 by up to 23%.

DOSA

MICRO 2023

Charles Hong, Qijing Huang, Grace Dinh, Mahesh Subedar, Yakun Sophia Shao

Formulates accelerator/compiler co-design as a differentiable optimization problem. By efficiently searching both together, we cut Gemmini’s energy-delay product by 45% in RTL simulation.

VAESA

ISPASS 2022

Qijing Huang, Charles Hong, John Wawrzynek, Mahesh Subedar, Yakun Sophia Shao

We train an autoencoder to turn discrete chip configurations into a continuous search space. Bayesian optimization finds near-optimal designs there with up to 85% fewer samples than in the original space.
